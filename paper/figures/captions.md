## Figure 1 — `fig1_overview.pdf` (full width)

**VoxParity at a glance, and its headline: all 28 systems err toward the words when the audio calls for protection.** (a) The 183 scenarios by cue type and sector (dot area = scenarios), each cue type with one example of what the audio adds and the action the rule then requires. (b) Each system's rate of carrying out the routine request on protective calls (y) against its rate of over-reacting on clean calls (x); marker shape gives serving mode. Every system sits above the dotted equal-rates line: pooled, 41% [36%, 46%] against 12% [9%, 15%], and for the four leading systems 34% against 18%. Reference points with 95% intervals: the words-only cascade and the volunteer players, the only point on or across the line.

## Figure 2 — `fig5_facts_feelings.pdf` (full width)

**Told the cue, agents act on the facts of a call more readily than on the caller's state; describing the voice narrows the gap without closing it.** (a) Told the cue in one line (for emotional delivery, a single label), gemini-3.7-flash on its own audio reaches 1.00 on environmental sound and 0.97 on a second voice but 0.59 [0.51, 0.67] on emotional delivery; across all six conditions (its own audio, the exact transcript, the Whisper transcript, and three text-only models on the Whisper transcript) emotion stays lowest, at 0.48–0.62, against 0.73–1.00 and 0.78–0.97. Hollow = the same model and input without the note; lines = 95% CIs, clustered by scenario. A sham note leaves credit unchanged (Figure A7). (b) Credit on emotional-delivery calls when the cue note describes the voice as rendered (filled) and when it gives the emotion label only (hollow), on identical calls: the description adds +0.07 to +0.18 across the five model and input rows (gemini-3.7-flash on its own audio 0.56 [0.48, 0.65] to 0.66 [0.58, 0.74]), while credit on other cues and on clean calls moves by −0.09 to +0.02. Emotion stays below the other cues (tick) in every row; for gemini-3.7-flash the gap narrows from 0.40 to 0.30 (own audio) and 0.35 to 0.26 (exact transcript). Emotional delivery here excludes sarcasm (n = 117 calls); on audio, the two notes were served through different routes (gemini-3.7-flash: OpenRouter and the Gemini API; Qwen3.8-Omni: its label-only run mixes OpenRouter and DashScope). Players are not drawn: neither panel uses their calls.

## Figure 3 — `fig2_leaderboard.pdf` (full width); Interspeech: `is_fig2_leaderboard.pdf` (panel b, column width)

**11 of 23 systems that can be tested against their own transcript act on the audio beyond the words-only null; the median system does not, and one of the seven production realtime agents with a transcript path does.** (a) Credit on the calls with an audible cue (95% CI), against the words-only cascade (0.34 [0.28, 0.39], dashed with grey band) and the players (0.61 [0.50, 0.73] on the calls they answered; solid line, edged band). (b) Gain over the null: how much a system's actions change between its audio and its own transcript, minus the same change for the cascade; for the 5 systems with no transcript input (lower block), audio credit minus the cascade's. Filled = clears the null after Holm correction; hollow = does not; ▼ = significantly below the null. The light-blue underlay is the range when each scenario family is left out in turn; the grey band spans the null under two other text models. Shape = serving mode.

## Figure A1 — `figA1_robustness.pdf`

**The leaderboard does not depend on which text model defines the null or on the legacy items.** Gain over the null per system (95% CI; filled = clears after Holm in its family) against the gpt-oss cascade (paper, 11 of 23 testable systems clear), a Sonnet 5 replay (10), a DeepSeek-V4-Pro replay (11), and gpt-oss without the 19 legacy LLM-drafted items (11). Rows as in Figure 3.

## Figure A2 — `figA4_sectors.pdf`

**Players out-score the words-only cascade in 12 of 14 sectors, and the leading system changes from sector to sector (11 different leaders).** Mean credit on calls with an audible cue by sector (columns, count of such calls in brackets, same order as Figure 1) for players (tool-selection credit on the subset of cells they answered, as few as 2 in a sector), the words-only cascade and the 28 systems (ordered by overall credit on these calls). ‡ = fewer than 5 scenarios (Collections, Government): read with care. Values: paper/figures/data/sector_by_system.json.

## Figure A3 — `figA_parity_by_cue.pdf` (half width); also Interspeech `is_fig1_axes.pdf`

**By cue type, the median system sits near the words-only cascade while the players sit above it on 5 of 7.** Credit on calls with an audible cue, per cue type: players (95% CI), gemini-3.7-flash (the highest-credit system, fixed on every row), the median of the 28 systems, and the words-only cascade (dashed; grey span from cascade to players). The median system is within 0.06 of the cascade on 5 of 7 cue types. gemini-3.7-flash is ahead of the players on second voice, disfluency / silence and masked word, level on speaker age and behind on emotional delivery, environmental sound and sarcasm; no single cue-type gap excludes zero with players resampled, and the gains and losses offset (overall −0.02 [−0.15, +0.12]). Rows with fewer than ten calls are drawn lighter.

## Figure A4 — `fig4_asymmetry.pdf` (full width); Interspeech: `is_fig4_asymmetry.pdf` (column width)

**Every group of systems errs toward the words, and the words-only cascade most of all.** Over-reaction on clean calls (left) and unsafe execution on protective calls (right): the four leading systems 18% [14%, 24%] / 34% [28%, 41%]; all 28 systems 12% [9%, 15%] / 41% [36%, 46%]; words-only cascade 15% [10%, 22%] / 58% [50%, 66%]; players, for reference, 21% [11%, 33%] / 18% [8%, 31%] (item and player resampled). Group rates average member systems per call, bootstrapped over scenarios. Figure 1b gives each system's own operating point.

## Figure A5 — `fig3_heard.pdf` (full width); Interspeech: `is_fig3_heard.pdf` (summary rows, column width)

**Pooled over rule modes, systems act on heard emotion less often than on other heard cues; within rule mode only gemini-3.7-flash's gap is significant.** Share of right actions when the system's own perception question shows it missed the cue (hollow) and when it heard it (filled), split into emotional delivery and all other cues. The four leading systems pooled (bold): 0.58 [0.48, 0.68] on heard emotion against 0.70 [0.62, 0.77] on other heard cues; all 27 systems with a perception answer pooled: 0.42 [0.35, 0.49] against 0.52 [0.46, 0.57]. Emotional cues come more often from scenarios that leave the rule unstated (58 of 98 against 13 of 66 environmental, second-voice, speaker and masked-word cues); stratified by stated rule, the gap over all systems and the whole bank is −0.04 [−0.13, +0.05] (pooled −0.10 [−0.18, −0.01]), while gemini-3.7-flash's stays at −0.20 [−0.35, −0.04]. Players (0.72 [0.57, 0.84] and 0.72 [0.53, 0.84]; they saw the scenario and the menu but not the stated policy) are drawn as the vertical band for reference; the comparison with them is suggestive only (Appendix A.8.4). Cells are the protocol-grounded calls the players also answered. Points drawn faint rest on fewer than 15 heard or missed calls. Shape = serving mode.

## Figure A6 — `figA2_taxonomy.pdf`

**Every system with a perception answer leaves 15% to 50% of the calls with an audible cue heard and not acted on; for 16 of 27, cues heard and not acted on outnumber cues not heard.** Share of the 206 calls with an audible cue per system: correct, heard but not acted on, heard and acted wrongly, not heard (perception from the system's own answer to a perception question), and calls whose perception answer names none of the options, counted as missing rather than as not heard (144 for Qwen3.5-Omni-Flash RT, 52 for MiMo-V2.6-Flash and 51 for Phi-4-multimodal). Sorted by the heard-not-acted share (right margin; median 31%). 27 systems with a perception answer.

## Figure A7 — `figA6_cue_note.pdf` (half width)

**Naming the cue in a one-line label nearly solves scene, speaker and masked-word cells but lifts emotional delivery only to about 0.6.** Cue-bearing credit with a note that names the cue (filled) vs a sham note (hollow), by axis, for gemini-3.7-flash on its own audio and for gpt-oss-120b on Whisper transcripts. With the note, gemini-3.7-flash reaches 0.59 [0.51, 0.67] on emotion cells and 0.98 [0.94, 1.00] on scene and second-voice cells. For emotional delivery this note is the label only; describing the voice as rendered lifts gemini-3.7-flash on its own audio to 0.66 [0.58, 0.74] on emotional-delivery calls, sarcasm excluded (Figure 2b). Axis groups follow experiments.json (scene includes second voice).

## Figure A8 — `figA3_test_information.pdf` (half width)

**Calls with an audible cue carry the bank's information where the systems and players are.** 2PL test information over ability (strict pass): all cells (peak at θ -0.2), calls with an audible cue (peak +0.3) and neutral cells (peak -1.6). Top strip: each system's θ (shape = serving mode). Dotted line: players, from the selection-basis refit (approximate). Marginal reliability over systems 0.985.
